The Disease

Basic Disease Information

Pompe disease is an inherited lysosomal storage disease: changes in GAA can reduce acid alpha-glucosidase activity, disrupting the breakdown of glycogen inside lysosomes.

◉

Name of the Disease

Pompe disease is also called glycogen storage disease type II (GSD II) or acid maltase deficiency. Johannes Cassianus Pompe described the condition in 1932; later work connected it to lysosomal deficiency. The historical milestones in the supplied summary are 1932, 1954, and 1963.

Taverna et al. (2020)
◌

Affected Organelle

The lysosome is a membrane-bound cell compartment that breaks down and recycles material. When GAA activity is deficient, glycogen is not adequately broken down in lysosomes and can accumulate, particularly affecting muscle tissue; liver and heart involvement are also described.

MedlinePlus Genetics · NORD · Cleveland Clinic
↻

Normal Organelle Function

Lysosomes contain enzymes that digest and recycle cellular material. Acid alpha-glucosidase (also called acid maltase) works in the acidic lysosomal environment and hydrolyzes α-1,4 and α-1,6 bonds in glycogen, releasing smaller sugar molecules for cellular use.

⌘

Gene Responsible

The GAA gene is located at chromosome 17q25.3. The supplied summary describes 20 exons, with the first non-coding, and hundreds of reported variants; counts differ by source and catalogue, so a single total is not asserted.

Taverna et al. (2020) · Peruzzo et al. (2019)
◇

Protein Produced

GAA encodes acid alpha-glucosidase, a 952-amino-acid protein. The supplied materials describe N1 (residues 80–136), N2 (137–346), a catalytic barrel (347–726), C1 (727–820), and C2 (821–952). These regions contribute to the mature enzyme's structure and function.

⌁

Symptoms & Affected Systems

Pompe disease can affect muscle strength and function. Features in the supplied summary include muscle weakness, infantile hypotonia, hepatomegaly, and—particularly in infantile-onset disease—cardiomegaly and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Respiratory decline is described in late-onset disease.

⇄

Inheritance Pattern

Pompe disease follows an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern and is associated with disease-causing variants in both copies of GAA. See the dedicated Cause of Mutation section for more detail.

Continue to Cause of Mutation.
⌛

Two Broad Onset Types

Infantile-onset Pompe disease (IOPD) may present in the first months of life and is associated in the supplied sources with cardiac involvement. Late-onset Pompe disease (LOPD) may begin in childhood or adulthood and is associated with limb-girdle weakness and respiratory decline.

See Disease Types for the comparison.

Genetics · Cause of disease

Cause of Mutation

Pompe disease is associated with disease-causing changes in the GAA gene. The inheritance pattern and the effect of a specific variant are related, but they are not the same question.

FIG. 07 / INH

Autosomal recessive inheritance

A person is affected when disease-causing variants are present in both copies of GAA, typically one inherited from each parent. A person with one altered copy may be a carrier and generally does not have Pompe disease.

HRSA · RUSP information

FIG. 08 / VAR

Different variants, different effects

Reported GAA variants include single-base substitutions and deletions. Depending on the change, the enzyme may be produced in smaller amounts, fold or traffic differently, or have reduced activity. The effect is variant-specific.

Peruzzo et al. (2019) · Taverna et al. (2020)

FIG. 09 / IMP

From gene change to glycogen storage

When functional acid alpha-glucosidase activity is insufficient, lysosomal glycogen breakdown is impaired. Glycogen can accumulate inside cells, with muscle and other tissues affected as described in the disease overview.

Disease Types

One Condition, Different Onset Patterns

Pompe disease is commonly discussed as infantile-onset and late-onset forms. The descriptions below reflect the supplied educational summary and are not a way to predict an individual course.

IOPD · Infantile-onset

Symptoms may appear in the first months of life

The supplied material describes hypotonia, feeding difficulty, cardiomegaly, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy among features associated with infantile-onset Pompe disease.

  • Cardiac involvement is an important feature in the described presentation.
  • Newborn screening studies report cases detected before or around symptom onset.
  • Presentation and progression vary; this summary cannot assess an individual.
LOPD · Late-onset

Symptoms may begin in childhood or adulthood

The supplied material describes limb-girdle muscle weakness and respiratory decline. Cardiac involvement is generally less prominent in the summary compared with IOPD.

  • Onset and progression can vary between people.
  • Screening programmes may identify individuals with later-onset disease.
  • Published detection estimates depend on the population and screening protocol.

These are broad educational categories, not diagnostic criteria. Clinical features and age at onset should be interpreted by a healthcare professional.

Genetics & Inheritance

How GAA Variants Affect the Lysosome

Pompe disease is associated with pathogenic variants in the gene encoding lysosomal acid alpha-glucosidase.

⌘

The GAA gene

GAA is located at chromosome 17q25.3. The supplied summary describes 20 exons, with the first non-coding, and reports hundreds of documented variants (reported counts vary across sources).

Taverna et al. (2020) · Peruzzo et al. (2019)
⇄

Autosomal recessive inheritance

The disease follows an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern. A family’s specific variant findings and recurrence considerations require individualized discussion with a qualified genetics professional.

◉

From variant to glycogen

Reduced acid alpha-glucosidase activity impairs lysosomal glycogen breakdown. Glycogen accumulation can affect cells and tissues, with skeletal and cardiac muscle among the clinically important sites described in the supplied sources.

Clinical Context

Cases, Forms & Real-World Detail

Concrete figures and clinical facts drawn from the cited literature.

⌁
16.7%

The mutation in real-world numbers

The c.2560C>T (p.Arg854*) variant was reported as 16.7% of alleles in a Brazilian infantile cohort and described as most common among African American patients in the supplied source summary.

Taverna et al. (2020)
⇢

Two forms, different trajectories

IOPD: onset in the first months may include hypotonia, feeding difficulty, cardiomegaly, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. LOPD: childhood or adult onset may involve limb-girdle weakness and respiratory decline, with the heart typically spared.

⌕

Diagnosis in practice

A diagnostic workup may involve dried blood spot screening, enzyme testing in leukocytes, fibroblasts, or muscle, and GAA sequencing. Methods and interpretation belong with qualified clinicians.

✳

Why early detection matters

The supplied materials describe treatment before symptoms as potentially helping preserve muscle and cardiac function. Individual treatment decisions require a specialist medical team.

Diagnosis

Evidence Builds Across Tests

The supplied material describes a pathway from screening signal to enzyme assessment and genetic analysis. Test selection and interpretation are clinical tasks.

01

Dried blood spot screening

Newborn screening can measure enzyme activity in a dried blood spot. A screening result is not, by itself, a complete diagnosis; follow-up testing may be needed.

Ficicioglu et al. (2020)
02

Enzyme activity assessment

The supplied notes list enzyme assays using leukocytes, fibroblasts, or muscle among diagnostic methods. The appropriate specimen and laboratory interpretation depend on the clinical setting.

03

GAA sequencing

Genetic analysis can help identify variants in GAA and support diagnostic evaluation. Results should be interpreted alongside enzyme findings and clinical information.

MedlinePlus Genetics

Why follow-up matters

Screening identifies people who may need further assessment. Confirmatory evaluation helps distinguish a screening result from a diagnosis and can inform what additional care is appropriate.

What this page does not provide

This educational page does not set assay cutoffs, interpret test results, or replace a diagnostic laboratory or specialist. Programme thresholds are source-reported only.

Mutation Analysis

From One Substitution to Cellular Impact

A simplified walkthrough of the featured c.2560C>T variant and its proposed consequences.

01

Type of change

A point substitution, specifically a nonsense mutation: C→T at position 2560 in the featured variant.

02

Did the codon change?

Yes. The illustrated coding codon changes from CGA to TGA (UGA in mRNA), introducing a stop signal; downstream codons are not translated.

03

Did the amino-acid sequence change?

Yes. Arginine at position 854 is replaced by a premature stop, yielding a protein described as truncated at 853 amino acids.

04

Effect on structure and function

The supplied material identifies C2 (residues 821–952) as important for folding and stability. It describes the truncated enzyme as unstable and non-functional, with glycogen accumulation and clinical manifestations as downstream effects.

This molecular walkthrough summarizes the supplied educational material. It is not a clinical interpretation of an individual's genetic test.

Treatment Landscape

Treatment, Access & Care Are Different Questions

The supplied notes identify alglucosidase alfa and emphasize the need to distinguish regulatory approval from availability and reimbursement.

✳

Enzyme replacement therapy

Alglucosidase alfa is listed in the supplied materials as an enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) with two U.S. regulatory records. ERT is intended to address reduced GAA enzyme function; product details, eligibility, risks, and care decisions must be discussed with a specialist.

Regulatory and therapy details vary by product and jurisdiction; source summary does not provide the full records.
＋

Multidisciplinary supportive care

Pompe disease can affect muscle, breathing, mobility, feeding, and cardiac function. The supplied materials do not specify a complete supportive-care protocol; care should be individualized by the treating team.

Keep three status questions separate

A therapy being authorized by a regulator does not establish whether it is currently supplied in a location or covered by a payer. Each status needs its own up-to-date, jurisdiction-specific source.
